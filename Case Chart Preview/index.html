Marbury v. Madison (1803)

FACTS Undelivered judicial commission; original mandamus request.

RULE Courts apply the Constitution over conflicting statutes; Congress cannot enlarge SCOTUS original jurisdiction.

EXAM CUE Right and remedy do not establish this court's jurisdiction.

SOURCE Constitution / qmnBMAegnWpMgDC_wxlf-1

Fletcher v. Peck (1810)

FACTS Georgia rescinds an earlier land grant.

RULE A state statute can be invalidated under the federal Constitution; rescinding the grant impaired contractual obligations.

EXAM CUE Federal judicial review reaches state legislation.

SOURCE Constitution / KJExrowZUuuFt-tTXspJ-3

Martin v. Hunter’s Lessee (1816)

FACTS Virginia refuses to follow a Supreme Court mandate about land title.

RULE SCOTUS may review state-court decisions on federal questions; uniform federal law requires review.

EXAM CUE Review attaches to the federal issue, not the identity of the state court.

SOURCE Constitution / KJExrowZUuuFt-tTXspJ-1

Cohens v. Virginia (1821)

FACTS State criminal conviction involving a claimed federal lottery authorization.

RULE SCOTUS appellate review of federal questions extends to state criminal judgments even when a state is a party.

EXAM CUE State participation does not automatically make every case exclusively original.

SOURCE Constitution / COWE_IKI9RGzQCRLrS6F-1; Constitution / C0olnXspH3CQYHDvb34H-19; Test Prep / HG-kfVQ57Oy37fDg51bK-10

Kline v. Burke Construction Co. (1922)

FACTS Parallel state and federal contract litigation.

RULE Lower federal courts need statutory jurisdiction within Article III's constitutional capacity.

EXAM CUE Constitutional capacity and statutory conferral are separate; jurisdictional control has constitutional limits.

SOURCE Constitution / nGnvCsMC4TkzJVelY6of-8

Ex parte McCardle (1869)

FACTS Congress repeals the statutory appellate habeas route during litigation.

RULE Court dismisses under the Exceptions Clause after Congress withdraws that appellate route.

EXAM CUE An alternative habeas route remained; the Court did not approve every conceivable jurisdiction strip.

SOURCE Constitution / 18EV3NXly3eGwHwJ9PCQ-4

United States v. Klein (1871)

FACTS Congress changes the legal effect of presidential pardons in pending claims.

RULE Congress may not use jurisdictional legislation to force unconstitutional judicial action or dictate results under existing law.

EXAM CUE Congress may change substantive law applicable to pending cases; Klein is not a ban on all outcome-affecting laws.

SOURCE Constitution / C0olnXspH3CQYHDvb34H-1

Trump v. CASA, Inc. (2025)

FACTS Universal injunctions against the birthright-citizenship order.

RULE At the stay stage, injunctions likely exceeded statutory equitable authority insofar as broader than necessary for complete relief to plaintiffs with standing.

EXAM CUE Complete relief can have effects beyond parties; class relief and APA remedies are distinct. CASA did not decide citizenship merits.

SOURCE Constitution / 9A686LOlzWrqTdymGBHn-2; Test Prep / HG-kfVQ57Oy37fDg51bK-1

STANDING & JUSTICIABILITY

Spokeo, Inc. v. Robins (2016)

FACTS Allegedly inaccurate personal information published by a people-search service.

RULE Concrete and particularized are separate requirements; a bare procedural statutory violation may lack concrete injury.

EXAM CUE SCOTUS remanded for concreteness analysis; it did not finally hold Robins had no concrete harm.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-10

TransUnion LLC v. Ramirez (2021)

FACTS Credit files wrongly flag consumers; only some reports reach third parties.

RULE A statutory violation alone does not establish concrete harm; disseminated false reports supported reputational injury.

EXAM CUE For damages, future risk alone did not establish concrete injury for the undisclosed-file group.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-12

Summers v. Earth Island Institute (2009)

FACTS Forest challenge continues after the concrete project dispute is settled.

RULE An organization needs an identified member facing concrete, imminent harm; someday plans are insufficient.

EXAM CUE Aesthetic injury can count, but connect it to a member, place, and likely visit.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-15

Allen v. Wright (1984)

FACTS Parents challenge IRS exemptions for discriminatory private schools.

RULE The asserted school-integration injury was too dependent on independent third-party choices for traceability.

EXAM CUE Stigmatic injury alone also failed for plaintiffs not personally denied equal treatment.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-15; Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-20; Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-33; Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-42; Constitution / ltZLwBbUF2npR7WB0Q-s-48

City of Los Angeles v. Lyons (1983)

FACTS Past police chokehold; request to enjoin future use.

RULE Past injury may support damages but does not alone establish a real and immediate threat for an injunction.

EXAM CUE Analyze standing separately for each requested remedy.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-24

Susan B. Anthony List v. Driehaus (2014)

FACTS Speakers fear enforcement of a law regulating campaign statements.

RULE Pre-enforcement standing can rest on intended arguably protected conduct, a statutory prohibition, and a credible enforcement threat.

EXAM CUE A plaintiff need not violate the law and await prosecution first.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-33

Zivotofsky v. Clinton (2012)

FACTS Passport birthplace statute conflicts with executive policy.

RULE Interpreting a statute and assessing its constitutionality is a judicial task, even in a foreign-affairs dispute.

EXAM CUE This is the justiciability decision; distinguish the 2015 merits decision.

SOURCE Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-38; Constitution / cJ5OH2NNq5MmNCnX_mFG-40; Constitution / Jr_ydECLnp-rl0KPYuwt-26

Rucho v. Common Cause (2019)

FACTS Partisan packing and cracking in congressional maps.

RULE Federal partisan-gerrymandering claims present political questions without judicially manageable constitutional standards.

EXAM CUE Do not equate partisan claims with all racial or voting-rights claims.

Hans v. Louisiana (1890)

FACTS Citizen sues the citizen's own state in federal court.

RULE State sovereign immunity extends beyond the Eleventh Amendment's literal out-of-state-plaintiff wording.

EXAM CUE Check consent, valid abrogation, structural surrender, and officer-suit routes.

Ex parte Young (1908)

FACTS State officer threatens to enforce an unconstitutional statute.

RULE Prospective relief against an enforcing state officer can stop an ongoing violation of federal law.

EXAM CUE Not a general route to retrospective damages from the state treasury; not merely a contempt doctrine.

SOURCE Constitution / 9A686LOlzWrqTdymGBHn-3; Test Prep / HG-kfVQ57Oy37fDg51bK-2

Galette v. New Jersey Transit Corp. (2026)

FACTS Bus-accident suits against NJ Transit in other states' courts.

RULE NJ Transit is not an arm of New Jersey entitled to its interstate sovereign immunity.

EXAM CUE Examine entity structure, state liability, and control; state-created does not automatically mean immune.

SOURCE Constitution / C0olnXspH3CQYHDvb34H-7; Test Prep / HG-kfVQ57Oy37fDg51bK-5

Fitzpatrick v. Bitzer (1976)

FACTS State employees seek Title VII damages for sex discrimination.

RULE Valid Fourteenth Amendment §5 legislation can abrogate state immunity.

EXAM CUE Require unmistakably clear intent and valid enforcement legislation; ordinary Article I power usually does not suffice.

FACTS National bank charter and Maryland's tax on the bank.

FACTS Homegrown wheat substitutes for purchases.

RULE Congress may aggregate the interstate effects of a class of local economic activity.

EXAM CUE Home consumption is not automatically outside commerce power.

FACTS Local restaurant buys food moving through interstate commerce.

RULE Congress may reach local restaurant discrimination based on the aggregate effects of that economic class.

FACTS Federal ban on gun possession near schools.

RULE Commerce power reaches channels, instrumentalities/persons/things, and activities substantially affecting commerce; this law exceeded it.

EXAM CUE Noneconomic possession plus attenuated economic consequences lacks a meaningful limiting principle.

FACTS Federal civil remedy for gender-motivated violence.

FACTS Locally cultivated medical marijuana used at home.

Jones v. Alfred H. Mayer Co. (1968)

FACTS Private refusal to sell a home because of race.

RULE Congress may rationally identify and legislate against badges and incidents of slavery under Thirteenth Amendment §2.

EXAM CUE Unlike Fourteenth Amendment §5, this power can reach private racial discrimination.

SOURCE Test Prep / HdfO-nH2aJV_gOckw8U0-14

City of Boerne v. Flores (1997)

FACTS RFRA applied to state/local land-use decisions.

RULE Fourteenth Amendment §5 is remedial; prophylactic legislation needs congruence and proportionality to constitutional injury.

EXAM CUE Congress enforces constitutional rights; it does not redefine their substance.

SOURCE Test Prep / HdfO-nH2aJV_gOckw8U0-11

Louisiana v. Callais (2026)

FACTS Louisiana creates a second majority-Black district to meet asserted VRA duties.

RULE Court invalidated the racial gerrymander and construed VRA §2 to fit Fifteenth Amendment enforcement limits, invoking congruence and proportionality.

EXAM CUE Distinguish Boerne's Fourteenth Amendment setting; review Callais's specific §2 framework for a redistricting question.

SOURCE Test Prep / HdfO-nH2aJV_gOckw8U0-12

Shelby County v. Holder (2013)

FACTS Old coverage formula determines jurisdictions subject to preclearance.

RULE VRA §4(b)'s differential coverage was invalid because it did not reflect current conditions justifying the burdens.

EXAM CUE The decision invalidated the coverage formula, not VRA §2 or the text of §5 preclearance itself.

New York v. United States (1992)

FACTS States must regulate radioactive waste or take title and liability.

RULE Congress cannot compel state legislatures to administer a federal regulatory program.

EXAM CUE Both alternatives were unconstitutional commands; permissible funding incentives are different.

Printz v. United States (1997)

FACTS Local officers ordered to run federal handgun background checks.

RULE Congress cannot conscript state/local executive officials to administer federal law.

EXAM CUE Temporary duties do not cure commandeering; distinguish direct regulation of the state's own activity.

Murphy v. NCAA (2018)

FACTS Federal law prevents states from repealing sports-betting prohibitions.

RULE An order to retain state prohibitions commandeers legislative authority just as an order to enact them does.

EXAM CUE Valid preemption regulates private actors pursuant to federal power; a bare order to a legislature is different.

Garcia v. San Antonio Metropolitan Transit Authority (1985)

FACTS Federal wage-and-hour law applied to a public transit employer.

RULE Generally applicable federal employment regulation can govern state/local employers.

EXAM CUE The state is regulated as an employer, rather than ordered to regulate its residents.

Reno v. Condon (2000)

FACTS Federal law restricts disclosure of DMV personal information.

RULE Congress may regulate a state's own database activities without requiring it to regulate private citizens.

EXAM CUE Contrast state-as-data-holder with conscripted state regulator.

FACTS President seizes steel mills during the Korean War.

RULE Seizure lacked authority; Jackson's concurrence organizes power by congressional authorization, silence, or opposition.

CORRECTIONS AND SOURCES

The new case cards use qualified formulations where the original notes overstate or mislabel a rule. Original notes are preserved.

Supremacy Clause quotation

Constitution labels the Tenth Amendment reservation of powers as Article VI supremacy text. Article VI instead makes the Constitution, valid federal laws, and treaties supreme over contrary state law.

Article III labels

Article III §2 cl.1 defines categories of judicial power, not exclusively appellate jurisdiction. Clause 2 separates SCOTUS original and appellate jurisdiction. Cohens prevents treating every state-party case as exclusively original.

Spokeo and Klein

Spokeo remanded for a separate concreteness analysis; it did not finally reject Robins’s injury. Klein does not prohibit Congress from changing substantive law applicable to pending litigation.

McCardle and Young

McCardle left an alternate habeas route intact but did not decide all constitutional limits on jurisdiction stripping. Young allows certain prospective federal-law suits against officers; its core is not judicial contempt.

CASA and relief

CASA concerns the scope of statutory equitable authority at the stay stage. Relief may reach beyond named parties when needed to give plaintiffs complete relief. It did not hold that class actions are the only possible broad remedy.

Tax and spending language

A payment need not be labeled a tax to be sustained under the taxing power. Conditional spending concerns grants/funding conditions, not just enforcement of ordinary government contracts.

National Pork Producers

The decision rejected automatic invalidation based merely on out-of-state effects. Its fragmented Pike analysis should not be compressed into a universal rule resolving every extraterritorial regulation.

Limits on this review

The case scope comes from your chart, not a class syllabus or lecture history. Non-case topics remain important. The audit compares your two pages; it does not certify that either page covers the full course.
