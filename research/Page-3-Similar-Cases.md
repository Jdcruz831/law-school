# Page 3: similar-fact case research

Research date: September 27, 2026. Legal sources: Westlaw only. Record facts are taken from your existing Page-3 and its JA citations; they were not independently re-audited for this supplement. Original pages are preserved. The diagram additions are on “Page-3 — Similar Cases.”

These are potential authorities and comparisons, not findings that Mission City is liable. State-law cases are persuasive analogies for this fictional Columbia problem, not federal holdings. Check any assignment cutoff before using the May 2026 Baker decision.

## Important gaps and treatment

- Wrong-address entry helps distinguish a genuine emergency, but does not itself establish a federal taking. Address isolated trespass versus appropriation and public necessity.
- Identify final policymaking authority and causation; a refusal to pay is not automatically ratification of the raid.
- Restitution needs identifiable City benefit and its own source of law; repair costs alone prove owner loss.
- Westlaw flags Wegner and Steele as declined to follow by Hamen; Hamen was read and is included as contrary authority. It does not overrule Minnesota or Texas law.
- Hartsfield is distinguished by White v. McLain (11th Cir. Apr. 19, 2016); that later opinion was not reviewed for this supplement. Use Hartsfield’s actual verification facts, not a categorical wrong-address rule.
- Edwards and the 2026 Baker opinion displayed no negative-treatment banner when read. This is not an exhaustive citing-reference review.
- Earlier Supreme Court authorities and the 2023 Baker opinion were verified in the prior Westlaw report; their qualifications remain applicable. Baker 2026 is unpublished and concerns Texas law.

## Fifth Amendment / police destruction

Wegner v. Milwaukee Mutual Insurance Co., 479 N.W.2d 38, 39–42 (Minn. 1991). SUPPORTIVE STATE-LAW ANALOGY. SWAT tear gas and flash-bangs damaged an innocent owner’s house; the city denied reimbursement. The court required compensation under Minnesota’s constitution. Similar damage, innocent owner, and denial; unlike this record, an armed suspect actually occupied the house. Its state “damaged” clause is broader than the federal text. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I5c3af345ff6411d9bf60c1d57ebc853e/View/FullText.html)

Baker v. City of McKinney, 84 F.4th 378, 383–88 (5th Cir. 2023). ADVERSE FEDERAL ANALOGY. Armored vehicles, explosives, and gas damaged an innocent owner’s home during a hostage emergency. No compensation where destruction was objectively necessary to prevent imminent harm. Distinguish the wrong address and disputed necessity here; Baker rejected a blanket police-power exception. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Id3b7acb068a011eea76695209c33e2ad/View/FullText.html)

UPDATE: Baker v. City of McKinney, No. 25-40396, 2026 WL 1453172, at *1, *3–4 (5th Cir. May 22, 2026) (unpublished), affirmed recovery under the Texas Constitution. It does not reverse the 2023 federal holding. Use only if the assignment permits post-record authorities. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ic724f4f0562611f19bdcd38b08cd9e81/View/FullText.html)

## Municipal liability / official decision

Pembaur v. City of Cincinnati, 475 U.S. 469, 472–73, 480–85 (1986). Police forced entry into a doctor’s clinic to execute arrest process for employees after the county prosecutor ordered entry. A single decision by an authorized final policymaker can be policy. Similar official decision and forced entry; unlike the Council’s later denial, the prosecutor’s order preceded and directed the entry. Identify final authority over compensation and connect the decision to the claimed injury. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I618c8aa09c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

Knick v. Township of Scott, 588 U.S. 180, 185 (2019). A town required public access to a private cemetery. An unpaid taking creates an immediate § 1983 claim without a prior state compensation lawsuit. Timing analogy, not police-damage precedent: the later Council vote cannot simply be assumed to be the first moment of deprivation. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I06fa8dbd941811e9b22cbaf3cb96eb08/View/FullText.html)

## Overall § 1983 elements

Hartsfield v. Lemacks, 50 F.3d 950, 951–56 (11th Cir. 1995), as amended June 14, 1995. Police used a battering ram at the wrong house despite a warrant naming another address. The leader lost qualified immunity because he made no reasonable verification effort; following officers retained it. Close wrong-address comparison, but this was a Fourth Amendment individual-liability ruling, not a municipal Fifth Amendment compensation holding. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I81219252918111d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

Pembaur, 475 U.S. at 480–85, supplies the separate municipal-policy analysis. Do not infer City liability merely because its officers performed the raid. Each § 1983 element and the specific constitutional deprivation must be established. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I618c8aa09c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

## Mission City as a person

Monell v. Department of Social Services, 436 U.S. 658, 660–62, 690–91 (1978). Female municipal employees challenged official maternity-leave policies. Local government bodies qualify as § 1983 persons, but employment alone does not create liability. Institutional analogy: Mission City’s admitted municipal status fits the defendant category; the underlying facts are employment, not a raid. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I6184263e9c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

## Meaning of person

Monell, 436 U.S. at 690: “Congress did intend municipalities and other local government units to be included among those persons to whom § 1983 applies.” Apply to the admitted municipal corporation. No factually matching police case is needed to resolve this classification. Keep personhood separate from policy, causation, and a constitutional violation. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I6184263e9c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

## When municipal liability attaches

Pembaur, 475 U.S. at 480: “municipal liability may be imposed for a single decision by municipal policymakers under appropriate circumstances.” The prosecutor’s instruction caused the forced clinic entry. Here, identify whether the Council finally decided compensation, and why that decision caused the uncompensated-taking injury. Part II-B’s discussion at 481–83 is a plurality. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I618c8aa09c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

Monell, 436 U.S. at 694, requires injury caused by execution of governmental policy or custom. A post-raid denial does not itself establish a policy directing wrong-address raids. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I6184263e9c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

## Property owner suing after police damage

Steele v. City of Houston, 603 S.W.2d 786, 788–89, 791–93 (Tex. 1980). Police allegedly burned an innocent owner’s house to force out escaped prisoners. The court allowed a Texas constitutional destruction claim to proceed and rejected governmental immunity. Similar innocent ownership and police destruction; actual fugitives were inside. It required intentional destruction for public use and permitted a great-public-necessity defense: “Mere convenience will not suffice.” Id. at 792. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I3d7b267ae79311d9bf60c1d57ebc853e/View/FullText.html)

Hamen v. Hamlin County, 955 N.W.2d 336, 342–48, ¶¶ 4–14, 23–32 (S.D. 2021). Officers damaged a mobile home despite reports that the suspect had left; he was found outside. The court rejected the state takings claim under a police-power/public-use distinction. Particularly useful adverse analogy because absence of the suspect did not itself produce compensation. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ie2fe88206c9911eba660be4ce62361b9/View/FullText.html)

## Extending liability to this City

Pembaur, 475 U.S. at 480–85. A government’s own authorized decision can support liability even if it occurs once. The affirmative pre-entry order is stronger causal evidence than a later refusal to pay. Explain that difference rather than treating the cases as identical. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I618c8aa09c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

City of Newport v. Fact Concerts, Inc., 453 U.S. 247, 250–53, 271 (1981). A city council canceled a concert license, producing civil-rights litigation. The council-action fact is analogous, but the holding relevant here is municipal immunity from § 1983 punitive damages; it does not establish that denial of a damage claim causes a taking. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I1d1a55069c9711d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

## Under color of state law

West v. Atkins, 487 U.S. 42, 49–57 (1988). A physician contracted to treat state prisoners acted under color of state law while performing that function. Functional analogy: Quick’s command of City SWAT and execution of a state warrant invoke official authority, even if exercised incorrectly. This proves the source of authority, not the City’s policy or liability. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I1786319b9c1f11d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

Hartsfield, 50 F.3d at 951–56, illustrates that a mistaken address during an official drug raid remains subject to § 1983 analysis. Its holding concerns individual Fourth Amendment liability, not an automatic municipal damages award. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I81219252918111d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

## Deprivation of a federal right

Hartsfield, 50 F.3d at 955–56. The leader had visited the correct house the previous day yet failed to verify the address; the homes were distinguishable. Compare Quick’s prior photograph and the houses’ differences. Distinguish the daylight, clearly marked numbers, and absence of exigency there from any supported nighttime or emergency facts here. It supports the Fourth Amendment route, not proof of a Fifth Amendment taking. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I81219252918111d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

Cedar Point Nursery v. Hassid, 594 U.S. 139, 159–61 (2021), distinguishes isolated tortious invasions from appropriations and recognizes traditional entry privileges. Explain why the particular damage is an appropriation, and whether the claimed privilege covered mistaken entry. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Iab37444ed36711eb9531b93dba0730fb/View/FullText.html)

## Private property / public use / no compensation

Wegner, 479 N.W.2d at 41–42, treated deliberate tear-gas damage while capturing a dangerous suspect as public use and required municipal compensation. Strong physical-damage and public-benefit analogy; its Minnesota constitutional holding cannot simply be substituted for federal law. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I5c3af345ff6411d9bf60c1d57ebc853e/View/FullText.html)

Steele, 603 S.W.2d at 791–93, required intentional destruction and public use. Distinguish an intentional decision to breach this particular house from a negligent choice of address. The court’s state-law analysis does not establish that every mistaken raid is a federal taking. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I3d7b267ae79311d9bf60c1d57ebc853e/View/FullText.html)

Baker, 84 F.4th at 388, supplies the opposing necessity analysis. The decisive comparison is the actual emergency and objective need for the particular destruction, not merely the amount of repair bills. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Id3b7acb068a011eea76695209c33e2ad/View/FullText.html)

## Issue 2 factual comparison

Hamen, 955 N.W.2d at 342–48, ¶¶ 4–14, 23–32, is the adverse “suspect not inside” comparison. Officers had seen the suspect enter earlier but later received reports he had fled; the home was damaged anyway. Compare that prior evidentiary link with the Nguyens’ entirely wrong address. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ie2fe88206c9911eba660be4ce62361b9/View/FullText.html)

Wegner, 479 N.W.2d at 39–42, is the supportive tear-gas/home-remediation comparison. Use alongside Baker, not as proof that all SWAT damage is compensable. Both Hamen and Wegner decide their own state constitutional clauses. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I5c3af345ff6411d9bf60c1d57ebc853e/View/FullText.html)

## Municipal causation

Pembaur, 475 U.S. at 484–85, tied the forced entry to the county prosecutor’s final instruction. Here distinguish (1) the police decision causing physical damage and (2) the Council decision withholding compensation. Establish final policymaking authority for the activity actually challenged. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I618c8aa09c1f11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

Knick, 588 U.S. at 185, places accrual at the unpaid taking, without first requiring a failed state compensation action. The later vote confirms refusal to pay, but a separate explanation is needed for why it satisfies Monell causation. Neither decision squarely holds that every post-incident refusal to reimburse satisfies Monell. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I06fa8dbd941811e9b22cbaf3cb96eb08/View/FullText.html)

## Citizenship

West, 487 U.S. at 48, states the deprivation and color-of-law elements. The Nguyens’ citizenship is admitted; no similar-fact citizenship case is necessary. Section 1983 also protects “other person[s] within the jurisdiction thereof,” so citizenship should not be framed as an absolute prerequisite. Concentrate research on the contested taking and municipal-policy issues. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I1786319b9c1f11d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

## Relief for constitutional claims

Baker, 2026 WL 1453172, at *1, *3–4 (unpublished), upheld $59,656.59 recovery for the same police raid under Texas law and rejected the City’s inadequately supported challenge to repair/replacement evidence. Close damages analogy; it does not adopt repair cost as an automatic federal measure. The decision also discusses election of remedies and avoiding double recovery under Texas law. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ic724f4f0562611f19bdcd38b08cd9e81/View/FullText.html)

Brown v. Legal Foundation of Washington, 538 U.S. 216, 235–37 (2003). In an IOLTA-interest taking, compensation turned on the owner’s pecuniary loss, not the government’s gain. Different facts, but useful to distinguish compensation from restitution. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I64fe1c3a9c9711d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

City of Newport, 453 U.S. at 271, bars § 1983 punitive damages against the municipality. Document compensable repair/remediation loss and any separately supported loss of use; do not assume all requested relief follows automatically. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I1d1a55069c9711d993e6d35cc61aab4a/View/FullText.html)

## Unjust enrichment

Edwards v. Lee’s Administrator, 96 S.W.2d 1028, 1029–33 (Ky. 1936). A cave operator sold tours that traversed a neighbor’s underground property without permission. The owner recovered the share of net profits attributable to that use. Closer property-use analogy than a trade-secret case: unauthorized access can confer a recoverable benefit. Important difference: repeated willful commercial use produced proven profit; the City’s mistaken raid produced no comparable receipts. This is state private-party law, not a holding recognizing federal restitution against a city. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ic9177ac5ee6f11d983e7e9deff98dc6f/View/FullText.html)

Ward v. Board of County Commissioners of Love County, 253 U.S. 17, 23–24 (1920), required a county to refund unlawful coercively collected taxes. It supports restitution against a government that actually received value; show what distinct benefit Mission City received rather than relabeling $112,678 in owner loss. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I0741605c9cbc11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

## Historical restitution basis

Ward, 253 U.S. at 23–24, imposed a refund obligation for money coercively collected by a county from federally protected Indian allotments. Historical government-defendant example; unlike the raid, identifiable money passed to the government. It does not decide whether § 1983 displaces this pleaded equitable theory. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I0741605c9cbc11d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)

Edwards, 96 S.W.2d at 1030–32, explains assumpsit, use and occupation, and an accounting for profits from unauthorized real-property use. Quote: “the unjust enrichment of the wrongdoer is the gist of the right to bring an action ex contractu.” Id. at 1031. Historical state-law analogy, not a federal municipal cause-of-action holding. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ic9177ac5ee6f11d983e7e9deff98dc6f/View/FullText.html)

## Restitution relief

Edwards, 96 S.W.2d at 1031–33, measured the defendant’s benefit attributable to the property; it allowed a share of net cave profits, excluded hotel profits, and rejected gross-profit recovery. Quote: “They are limited to the benefits accruing to the appellants.” Id. at 1032. Identify a defensible use/access value or actual avoided expense. The City’s failure to pay a disputed damage claim does not itself establish enrichment equal to the repair bill. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/Ic9177ac5ee6f11d983e7e9deff98dc6f/View/FullText.html)

Contrast Brown, 538 U.S. at 235–37: just compensation concerns the owner’s loss. Restitution and compensation require different valuation evidence. Missing here: proof of a measurable City benefit, applicable restitution law and municipal defenses, and an explanation why § 1983 does not foreclose the particular independent claim. [Westlaw](https://1.next.westlaw.com/Document/I64fe1c3a9c9711d9bc61beebb95be672/View/FullText.html)
